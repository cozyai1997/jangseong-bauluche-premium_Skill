# Evidence contract

## Data shape

Keep each record in `DEVELOPMENT_PROJECTS` with:

- stable `id`, display `number`, and managed `mediaKey`;
- official or accurately shortened `title`;
- qualified `status`;
- only source-backed `metrics`;
- a restrained `summary`;
- one or more descriptive HTTPS source links.

Freeze the collection. Update the JSDoc/TypeScript union, media mapping, tests, and administrator catalog together when adding an ID.

## Source quality

Prefer the agency responsible for the project, local government, council minutes, official press releases, procurement or planning records, and other primary records. Use a secondary article only as context, not as sole proof for a mutable number or status.

Open the exact page, not a search result. Ensure the label names the publisher and the supported fact. A development illustration is presentation, not evidence.

## Claim ladder

Preserve the source's epistemic strength:

- proposal or discussion → `검토`, `논의`, or `제안`;
- adopted plan or designation → `계획`, `지정`, or `추진`;
- scheduled target → `목표` or `예정`;
- modelled outcome → `추정` or `기대효과`;
- observed completion → use completion language only with current official proof.

Do not convert total modelled employment effects into direct hires. Do not imply a project guarantees demand, prices, returns, completion dates, budgets, or tenant commitments.

## Date and disclaimer

Attach dates to progress rates and other quickly changing statements. Maintain a section-level evidence cutoff and disclaimer explaining that names, scale, schedule, budget, design, and provision conditions may change and that projected economic effects do not guarantee employment or property appreciation.
